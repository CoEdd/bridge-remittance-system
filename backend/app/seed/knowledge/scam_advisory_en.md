# Advisory: tuition payment scams aimed at international students

> Sample content for the demo; the institution and providers are fictional.

## Fake payment agents offering a discount <!-- clause: fake-agents -->

Someone contacts you, often by chat, and says they are an official payment agent for your university. They offer to pay your tuition for you at a discount if you send the money to them first. Universities such as UTS and the University of Sussex publicly warn international students about this pattern. The tuition is never paid, or it is paid with stolen cards and later reversed, and you still owe the full fee.

## Pressure to pay quickly <!-- clause: urgency -->

Scammers set short deadlines such as "today only" or "within two hours" so that you do not have time to check with your university.

## Threats <!-- clause: threats -->

Messages that threaten your visa, your enrolment, deportation or arrest unless you pay at once are a warning sign. Universities do not collect fees this way.

## Personal bank accounts <!-- clause: personal-accounts -->

A request to pay tuition into an account in a person's name is a warning sign. Universities collect fees through their own official account or their student portal.

## Changed account details <!-- clause: changed-details -->

Be careful when a message or invoice says the receiving account has changed. Confirm the new details with the finance office using contact details you already trust, not the ones in the message.

## Requests for secrecy <!-- clause: secrecy -->

Being told not to tell the finance office, your family or your friends is a warning sign. A genuine offer does not need to be kept secret.

## What to do <!-- clause: what-to-do -->

Do not pay. Check with your university's finance office, pay only through the official channel, and keep the message as evidence.
